# Havenbrook — Presentation Script

~7-8 minutes, 3 speakers, 16 slides. PRESENT mode opens paused on purpose — advance with the **→** key when your part ends, and **let the camera land** on the building before you start talking about it (that's the point — it zooms for you). Don't read this word for word; know your bullets and say it like you built the thing (you did).

## ABHINAV — slides 1–5: the town and the care system

**Slide 1 — Title** *(camera: wide orbit of the whole town)*
"Hey, we're Abhinav, Dinesh, and Davi, and this is Havenbrook — a town we designed around a medical school for the Our Town Healthcare System project. What you're seeing behind me is the actual town — it's a real 3D map, and the camera's going to fly to each place as we talk about it. Twenty-six buildings, every one named, all inside a ten-million-dollar budget."

**Slide 2 — The town** *(camera: high aerial, pans across the districts)*
"Quick version of how it works: the med campus sits dead center and everything zones out from it. Healthcare runs along Wellness Way up north, shopping runs along Commerce Boulevard down south. Housing and the K-12 hold the west side, and the senior care block gets the quiet northeast corner. The rules we had to hit: at least seven healthcare facilities, at least three community locations, a real name on every building, and all of it under ten million."

**Slide 3 — Who lives here** *(camera: tours students' flats → family blocks → senior corner)*
"Our population is basically a college town. Half the residents are college students, so we put flats and a student health clinic right up against the campus. About a third are families — they cluster around the school district. And one in five is over sixty-five, so the senior living and the hospice sit together in the quiet part of town. Honestly, that 50-30-20 split is what decided the shape of the whole map."

**Slide 4 — Havenbrook University School of Medicine** *(camera: dives onto the quad)*
"This is the anchor — Havenbrook University School of Medicine. It's donated, so it's free and doesn't count against our budget, which is nice because it's also the biggest thing on the map. It's got the Marchand Medical Library and two lecture halls — Whitmore Anatomy and Caldecott Clinical Sciences — around one quad. The whole town exists because of this school, so it gets the center."

**Slide 5 — Thirteen ways to get care** *(camera: hospital → ambulance bay + student clinic → lab and rehab)*
"So, healthcare — we ended up with thirteen facilities, way over the seven required. Emergency care is Havenbrook General Hospital — that's what it's flying to now — with Garrison Medical Transport, the ambulance station, a block apart on Wellness Way. Everyday stuff is the Thacher Student Health Clinic by campus, Brookfield Family Physicians near the homes, Marigold Dental Studio, Parallax Optical, and the Meridian Diagnostics lab next to the hospital. Long-term care is Stoneleigh Rehab, the Ashwood Behavioral Health Center, Halcyon House Senior Living, and Stillpoint Hospice. Plus the County Health Department for public health and Innisfree Home Health for people who can't come in. The point is every demographic has something — students get the cheap clinic, families get pediatricians, seniors get the whole long-term row."

> → "Dinesh's got the neighborhoods."

## DINESH — slides 6–10: housing, community, seniors, shopping, layout

**Slide 6 — The Preserve at Havenbrook** *(camera: across the commons to the housing rows)*
"The Preserve at Havenbrook is our required housing development — also donated, also free. It's a real neighborhood: cottages, duplexes, townhouses, and the Preserve Commons Apartments in the middle. Students mostly take Scholar's Court and Scholar's Walk plus the University Lofts since they're closest to campus, and Midtown Flats covers downtown. Nobody's more than a short walk from campus or a bus route."

**Slide 7 — Community** *(camera: mall → Bardsley's/Olsen's → the Main Street row)*
"For the community side we built eleven locations. Errands: Havenbrook Commons Mall, Bardsley's Department Store, Olsen's Market for groceries, and Bellwether Pharmacy. Food: the Orchard Table — that's our farm-to-table spot — Mariposa Cantina for fast Mexican, and the Whippoorwill Coffeehouse, which is basically where the students live. Willow Creek Park has the pond, trails, and a bandshell. And then the boring-but-needed ones: the post office, the school district, and Quarry Hill Museum."

**Slide 8 — A place to grow old** *(camera: Halcyon House, then Stillpoint next door)*
"The east side is the senior block. Halcyon House is assisted living with a memory-care wing around a courtyard. Stillpoint Hospice is right next door — that was on purpose, so the hardest visits a family makes stay short and private. Innisfree Home Health keeps seniors in their own homes longer. The whole block sits on Sunset Ridge — farthest from traffic, closest to quiet."

**Slide 9 — Shopping and entertainment** *(camera: mall → Bardsley's → museum)*
"When people aren't at the doctor, this is where they end up. The Commons Mall anchors the whole Commerce corridor — it's the biggest single thing we bought. Bardsley's and Olsen's Market handle the everyday essentials. Quarry Hill Museum sits on the civic plaza for the rainy-day option, and Willow Creek Park plus the Whippoorwill cover the slower afternoons."

**Slide 10 — How we laid it out** *(camera: the grid, then dives down Wellness Way)*
"Here's why things are where they are. We laid the streets first — two arterials cross at the campus and eighteen named streets fill in the grid. The hospital and the EMS station share Wellness Way, so an ambulance is minutes from anywhere. Shops run along Commerce Boulevard because that's the traffic they want. And the rule we kept everywhere: every building is within two blocks of a through-street, so emergency response actually works."

> → "Davi — the downtown, the school, and the money."

## DAVI — slides 11–16: downtown, the school, the trades, the budget, the close

**Slide 11 — Haven downtown** *(camera: the old-town skyline and plaza)*
"This is the old part of town — the downtown. It's a walkable core where the campus, the hospital district, and the shops share a few busy blocks, and a student can literally go class, clinic, coffee without ever needing a car. The plaza and the skyline — Pinnacle Health Plaza, Foundry One — give the town a real center instead of just being a campus with houses around it."

**Slide 12 — Havenbrook Unified School District** *(camera: orbits the K-12 campus)*
"The school district anchors the southwest side — that's where the families are. It's not just a checkbox: the K-12 runs health-science electives and athletics that feed straight into the university pipeline, so a kid here can go all the way from kindergarten to med school without leaving town. And it sits inside the neighborhoods it serves, so kids walk instead of busing."

**Slide 13 — What we traded** *(camera: medical district, then the south side)*
"The big trade-off: because the university and the housing were donated, almost the whole budget went to facilities — so instead of one giant hospital campus we bought thirteen smaller ones that each cover a different need. We picked the mall over a second park since the Preserve already has green space. And we deliberately stopped at twenty-six buildings — we banked the leftover fifty thousand instead of forcing a twenty-seventh thing we didn't need."

**Slide 14 — The budget** *(camera: straight overhead while the ledger runs)*
"Here's the ledger. Healthcare was six-point-two-five million across thirteen sites — the hospital's the most expensive at one-point-five, the lab's the cheapest at a hundred grand. Community was three-point-seven across eleven sites, the mall being the big one at a million. Total: nine-point-nine-five million spent of ten million — fifty thousand left over — and the two biggest things on the map, the university and the Preserve, were donated so they cost us nothing."

**Slide 15 — Why it works** *(camera: back out to the sweeping aerial)*
"So why it works: students get the campus, the flats, the Thacher clinic, and the Whippoorwill — no car needed. Families get the school, Brookfield's pediatricians, Olsen's Market, and the park. Seniors get the full care loop — Innisfree home visits all the way to Stillpoint. And the whole thing lands under budget."

**Slide 16 — References** *(camera: slow orbit)*
"Numbers came from the class budget sheet, the demographic split is from Census QuickFacts, and the siting logic follows the American Planning Association. The town itself was built and rendered in Three.js — and if you want, we can actually drop inside it and walk around. Questions?"

## If they ask "why did you put X there"

- **Hospital** → center-north on Wellness Way = ambulance reach to every district, ER bay on its own apron
- **Senior block** → Sunset Ridge, the quietest corner, still on a through-street
- **School** → inside the family neighborhoods, not downtown
- **Mall / retail** → Commerce Blvd — retail wants the traffic
- **Student clinic** → faces the campus — walkable for the 50%
- **Behavioral health** → short walk from student housing — that's who's most likely to use it
- **Why 13 facilities instead of a mega-hospital** → coverage over scale; the 50/30/20 split needs different kinds of care, not more beds
